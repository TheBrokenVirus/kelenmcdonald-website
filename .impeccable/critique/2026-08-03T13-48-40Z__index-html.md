## Design Specificity Verdict

**LLM assessment**: The page is built on a generic three-part agency skeleton (hero → 3-up services → 3-up work grid → about → dark CTA), but it's held together by a genuinely specific conceptual thread: a drafting/blueprint metaphor — registration-mark corner crops, a faint drafting-grid background, mono-spaced eyebrows, copy verbs ("drafted," "sketch," "draft something together"), and wireframe thumbnails that draw themselves on hover. That motif ties directly to what's being sold (hand-drafted, non-templated sites) and would look wrong on an unrelated studio. It earns "specific," not "generic" — but see the deterministic scan below for a countervailing signal.

**Deterministic scan**: The detector's live-page pass (richer than the static CLI scan, since it reads computed styles) flagged 7 distinct pattern categories, 9 instances total: `kicker-above-heading` used 3× (the eyebrow-above-`<h2>` device appears on Services, Work, and Contact — a very common landing-page convention), `cream-palette` (the page's warm off-white background is a frequently-seen choice), `overused-font` ×2 (Fraunces at 24% of text, Inter at 36% — both are heavily-used Google Fonts), and `repeating-stripes-gradient` (the hero's drafting-grid background pattern). None of these are wrong in isolation, and the concept they serve is specific — but stacked together they're exactly the kind of common/templated surface choices that show up across many generic and AI-generated sites, which cuts against the "hand-built, not templated" pitch this business is making. Two more findings are functional, not stylistic, and are folded into Priority Issues below: `low-contrast` (eyebrow/tag label color) and `layout-transition`.

**Visual overlays**: Browser injection succeeded; the detector rendered a visible on-page overlay (banner + orange outlines) confirmed via desktop (1280px) and mobile (375px) screenshots in Assessment B.

## Overall Impression

The concept is real and well-executed at the CSS-authoring level — this is not a template with the serial numbers filed off. But the page currently fails its own pitch on the device class most visitors will use: the Work section's proof-of-craft thumbnails are invisible without a mouse hover, sections can go permanently blank if JS doesn't fire, and the mobile header visibly collides at common phone widths. For a one-person studio whose entire sales argument is "I build this by hand, carefully," these are the details a skeptical prospect will notice first — and they undercut the argument the site exists to make. The single biggest opportunity: make the Work section's proof render correctly with no interaction required, since that's the section doing the actual selling.

## What's Working

1. **The drafting metaphor is genuinely load-bearing** — crop marks, grid backdrop, mono-space eyebrows, and self-drawing wireframe thumbnails all reinforce one idea tied directly to the service being sold, not stock-agency dressing.
2. **A disciplined three-tier type system** (Fraunces for craft/emotion, Inter for body, IBM Plex Mono for functional labels) creates real hierarchy and doubles as a demonstration of typographic judgment for a type-literate buyer.
3. **Global `:focus-visible` styling** is present site-wide — a detail most portfolio sites skip, and it quietly reinforces the craft narrative (though it's undermined by the missing focus target on work-cards — see Priority Issues).

## Priority Issues

**[P0] Work-thumbnail artwork is invisible without hover — i.e., always invisible on touch**
- Why it matters: `stroke-dasharray:600; stroke-dashoffset:600` only resolves via `:hover`, with no touch/focus/default-visible fallback. Confirmed on a 390px viewport: all three case-study cards show only a stray vertical line where the illustration should be. This is the proof-of-craft section for a design-quality sale, and it looks broken on the device class most prospects will actually use.
- Fix: default the SVG strokes to drawn, gate the dash-draw novelty behind `@media (hover:hover)`, or trigger the draw-in via the page's existing IntersectionObserver on scroll instead of hover.
- Suggested command: `/impeccable adapt`

**[P0] Content can go permanently invisible if JS doesn't run**
- Why it matters: `.reveal{opacity:0}` is only flipped visible by JS adding `.in` — no `<noscript>` or CSS-only fallback. Affects the Services head, the entire services grid, the entire work grid, and the about grid. Any script error or blocked script silently blanks large parts of a page whose only job is to be seen.
- Fix: add a `<noscript>` override forcing `.reveal{opacity:1;transform:none}`, or invert the pattern so content is visible by default and JS only adds enhancement.
- Suggested command: `/impeccable harden`

**[P1] Systemic low-contrast label color — confirmed independently by both assessments**
- Why it matters: `--amber-deep:#C97F1D` text on `--paper:#F6F3EC` (the "WHAT I DO" eyebrow and all three service tags — Identity/Interface/Build) measures ~2.9:1, failing WCAG AA (needs 4.5:1). The design review flagged this manually; the detector's live-computed-style scan flagged the identical color pairing independently — this is the strongest-evidence issue in the report. It repeats across the label system, so it's a systemic accessibility failure on a page whose selling point is craftsmanship.
- Fix: darken the label color (or reuse `--ink-soft`) until it clears 4.5:1; reserve amber for larger/bolder decorative use only.
- Suggested command: `/impeccable audit`

**[P1] Mobile header collision at 375px**
- Why it matters: at 375px width, the "Start a project" nav-cta wraps to two lines and visibly crowds the "KM WEB DESIGN STUDIO" logo in the sticky header — confirmed via screenshot. Because the header is `position:sticky`, this crowded state persists for the entire scroll session on a common phone width.
- Fix: add a sub-720px breakpoint that shrinks or truncates the nav-cta, or stacks it, so it never wraps inside the header bar.
- Suggested command: `/impeccable adapt`

**[P2] Work cards signal "clickable" but aren't**
- Why it matters: `.work-card:hover` applies full elevation (translateY + shadow) — the standard "this is clickable" signal — to a plain `<article>` with no link or button inside, and no keyboard focus target either. Prospects most engaged by a case study hit a dead end exactly when interest peaks, and keyboard users can't reach the hover treatment at all.
- Fix: wrap each card in a link to a larger view/case detail, or drop the elevation treatment so it stops promising an interaction that isn't there.
- Suggested command: `/impeccable clarify`

## Minor Observations

- Anchor clicks push browser history entries (`scroll-behavior:smooth`), so "back" after in-page nav may take multiple presses to leave the page.
- SVG wireframe thumbnails have no `<title>`/`aria-hidden`/text alternative for screen readers (likely acceptable since surrounding copy already describes the work, but worth confirming).
- The Contact section and footer share nearly the same dark tone with only a 1px border between them — on a fast scroll they can read as one long block.
- "2026" as the project year on all three concept pieces, next to a tiny "Concept ·" prefix, is worth a glance-test to confirm it doesn't get misread as a future ship date.
- Detector-confirmed but low-stakes: Fraunces/Inter are both very commonly used Google Fonts, and the warm cream background is a frequently-seen palette choice — not wrong, just worth knowing these read as "safe" choices to a design-literate visitor.

## Questions to Consider

1. If all three Work examples are concept pieces, what actually proves you can ship a real, live, deployed site — is there room for even one working prototype link instead of three static mockups?
2. The page's whole argument is "hand-built, not templated" — but it currently ships with hover-only artwork invisible on touch and a mobile header collision. If a prospect notices either, does the copy's promise survive contact with the product?
3. Is a bare `mailto:` really the highest-converting ask for a small business owner on their phone mid-workday, or would a two-field form or scheduling link lower the activation energy at the exact moment you have their attention?
